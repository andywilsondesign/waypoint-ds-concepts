UX exploration 03

The Library and the Workbench

Three studies of one question: how to move between a calm, explorable design system and a focused place to decide what new product evidence means, without losing your bearings.

The assistant is simulated. The grouping, counts, suggestions and “wired to” checks are scripted to show the experience, not a working analysis.

A · Embedded workbench

The workbench unfolds inside the catalogue

Open study A

The library is a grid of specimen tiles. Opening a family unfolds its workbench under that row, pointing back at the tile, so the rest of the system stays on screen. Definition on the left; observed groups on the right, each opening into an inspector in place.

B · Focus workspace

A quiet index, then a dedicated split room

Open study B

The library is a typographic index with a one-line map of every family. A family opens into a full workspace: approved on the left, observed on the right, compared property by property with visual crops. The map rides along in the top bar, so you always know where you are.

C · Sorting table

Piles, a belt and buckets shaped like the component

Open study C

The library is a board of family cards; unsorted evidence piles up behind the card it probably belongs to. At the table, piles ride a belt above buckets for the definition, variants, brand themes, exceptions, other families and “not sure”. Drag, or pick up and place. A live library entry shows the result before you commit.

How volume stays manageable

Hundreds of instancesInstances are grouped by what differs. Matching ones are filed and only counted. A designer sees a handful of groups, each with one representative and any outliers.
20 familiesThe library answers “what needs me?” in one sentence and one filter, or one map strip in B. Settled families stay quiet.
Several themesBrands are values of the same tokens. One switch re-themes everything; themed instances confirm a mode rather than creating families.
Owners and teamsAgreed changes carry the owning team. B’s “group by owner” turns the same library into each team’s to-build list.
Bulk with careEvery placement states its scope (“11 of 12”) and lets an outlier stay open, so a bulk decision never hides uncertainty.

Strongest patterns

Workbench anchored to its tile (A)Investigating in place, with a visible tie back to the library, removes the “where am I?” cost almost entirely.
Property crops with wiring labels (B)Each property shown as a small visual (corner, height, swatch) with its token or “hardcoded” beside it. The clearest way to separate how it looks from what it is connected to.
Family map strip (B)One line, one cell per family, coloured by state. Works as overview, navigation and orientation at once.
Buckets shaped like the component (C)The options are the component’s own anatomy, so placing evidence visibly builds the system instead of filling a form.
Piles behind library cards (C)Unsorted evidence lives where it probably belongs, so the library itself shows where judgement is needed.
Define the variant, don’t copy the instance (all)Creating Large asks for its intended tokens, and the hardcoded fill is not carried over.
Scope with an exit for outliers (all)“Applies to 11 of 12, Refund left open” keeps bulk decisions honest.
